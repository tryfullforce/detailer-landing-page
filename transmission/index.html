Is this about your truck?

If you drive a 2007 or newer GM truck or SUV with a V8, and on many 2014 and newer trucks the V6 too, your engine very likely has GM's cylinder shut off system. GM calls it Active Fuel Management (AFM) on earlier trucks and Dynamic Fuel Management (DFM) on 2019 and newer.

Two ways to know for sure. On many of these trucks the info screen in the gauge cluster shows when it happens, as a V4 or ECO message depending on the year and trim. Or run your make, model and year through the fit check further down this page.

If most of your miles are steady highway miles, your truck spends a big part of its life in that 4 cylinder mode. That is exactly when the heat described below happens.

What is heating the fluid

  1. The engine turns off 4 of its 8 cylinders.When you cruise light, GM's cylinder shut off system, called AFM (DFM on newer trucks), runs your V8 on 4 cylinders to save fuel.
  2. A V8 on 4 cylinders shakes.You have probably felt it, a low rumble through the floor at highway speed.
  3. The engine and transmission are allowed to slip.Normally the torque converter locks them together at cruising speed so nothing slips. In 4 cylinder mode that connection is allowed to slip a little on purpose, which soaks up the shake before it reaches your seat.
  4. Slip is friction, and friction is heat.That heat goes into the transmission fluid, on the easiest driving the truck does. Steady speed, light throttle, mile after mile.

Why a flush does not solve it

New fluid goes into the same system, gets heated the same way, and comes out dark again. The service schedule was never the problem. The thing heating the fluid is still there.

Before AFM, GM trucks did not shut cylinders off, so nothing told the transmission to slip while cruising. That is why older GM trucks usually have fluid that still looks like fluid.
