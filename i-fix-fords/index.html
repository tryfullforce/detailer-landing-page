How GM does it

Since 2007 GM has put Active Fuel Management in its V8s. AFM, or DFM on the newer ones.

When you’re cruising, the computer shuts off half the cylinders.

To do that, the lifters on those cylinders have a small locking pin that engages and releases. Hundreds of times a drive.

GM’s own service bulletins on lifter failures in these engines point at that pin. Here is the wording from one of them.

From GM’s own service bulletin (PIP4568Q)

Tick noise and misfires on the AFM cylinders (1, 4, 6 and 7) “may be the result of internal locking pin damage, which may occur if the response time of an AFM lifter unlocking event is decreased due to low oil pressure, oil aeration, internal engine sludge,” or wear in the lifter, the VLOM, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe.

No. It doesn’t repair a lifter that’s already worn. If your truck is ticking or misfiring, get it looked at first. It stops the switching on an engine that is still healthy.
