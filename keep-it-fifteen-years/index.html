The part with a lot of work to do

Since 2007, GM has put Active Fuel Management in its V8s. AFM, or DFM on the newer ones.

At light throttle, the computer shuts off half the cylinders to save fuel.

To do that, the lifters on those cylinders have a small locking pin that engages and releases. Hundreds of times every drive.

GM has published its own service bulletins on lifter failures in these engines. Here is the wording from one of them.

From GM’s own service bulletin (PIP4568Q)

Tick noise and misfires on the AFM cylinders (1, 4, 6 and 7) “may be the result of internal locking pin damage, which may occur if the response time of an AFM lifter unlocking event is decreased due to low oil pressure, oil aeration, internal engine sludge,” or wear in the lifter, the VLOM, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe.

Read the bulletin on NHTSA’s site

Nothing on the tractors I service switches half its engine off to save fuel.

That’s not a knock on GM. It’s a trade they made. A little fuel economy, for a part that has a lot of work to do.

And on a truck somebody plans to keep for fifteen years, that’s a lot of work.
