Is this about your truck?

If you drive a 2007 or newer GM truck or SUV with a V8, and on many 2014 and newer trucks the V6 too, your engine very likely has GM's cylinder shut off system. GM calls it Active Fuel Management (AFM) on earlier trucks and Dynamic Fuel Management (DFM) on 2019 and newer.

Two ways to know for sure. On many of these trucks the info screen in the gauge cluster shows when it happens, as a V4 or ECO message depending on the year and trim. Or run your make, model and year through the fit check further down this page.

Many of these trucks were built with mounts designed to soak up the extra shake from that 4 cylinder mode, which is why those mounts carry a bigger load than the mounts on older trucks.

The buzz at the light is what most owners notice first.

Why these mounts wear out

  1. Motor mounts are the pads that hold the engine in place.On most vehicles they sit there and last. Most owners never think about them.
  2. AFM trucks use special mounts.On trucks with Active Fuel Management (DFM on newer ones), the mounts are built to change how stiff they are, so they can soak up extra shaking.
  3. A V8 on 4 cylinders vibrates harder.When the engine drops to 4 cylinders to save fuel, it shakes more than it should. The active mounts are there to cancel that out.
  4. So they work extra, all day.Every time the engine drops into 4 cylinder mode, the mounts take on the extra job. That wears them out faster, and a new mount goes right back into the same job.

A new mount only buys time if the job it is doing has not changed.

Why a repair keeps coming back

The new mount starts cancelling the same extra vibration and wears the same way. It happens on trucks with careful owners and low miles. It is not abuse. It is the extra workload.

Before AFM, GM trucks did not shut cylinders off, so they did not need mounts that fight vibration. A repair that comes back usually means nobody changed the reason it failed.
