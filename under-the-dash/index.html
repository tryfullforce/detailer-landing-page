What’s built into both of those trucks

Marcus and Dennis had the same system inside their engines. Here’s what it is, in plain words.

Half the engine takes a break

While you cruise at a steady speed, GM’s Active Fuel Management (AFM, called DFM on newer trucks) shuts off some of the cylinders to save a little fuel.

A tiny pin does the switching

The lifters on those cylinders carry a small locking pin. It locks or releases every time the system switches, hundreds of times on a normal drive.

Pins wear. Sometimes they let go

When one gives out, the lifter can collapse and start grinding into the camshaft. Most of these engines get through it. Some don’t, and nobody can tell you ahead of time which ones.

Example shown for a V8. V6 versions work the same way with fewer cylinders.

That’s the whole reason a small module can matter. It takes the switching out of the picture.

What it does. And what it doesn’t.

What FullForce does

  • Tells the computer to keep every cylinder firing
  • Stops the locking pin from cycling
  • Leaves the truck’s computer (the ECU) alone. Nothing is reprogrammed

What it doesn’t do

The 60 second dash check

You don’t need tools, a scanner or a mechanic to see where your truck stands.

  • Sit in the driver’s seat and look down and to the left, near your knee, just under the dash. The OBD2 port is a wide, flat, 16 pin socket with a squared off shape.
  • Empty means factory. Nothing is plugged in and the switching system runs as GM built it.
  • Something already there? Insurance dongles and trackers use this port too. That isn’t FullForce, and it can stay or go.
  • Write down two things. Your year is on the driver door jamb sticker. Your engine size (4.3, 5.3, 6.0 or 6.2) is in the owner’s manual or on the underhood label.
  • Run them through the checker below. It takes about ten seconds.

Nothing about this check involves opening or removing anything.
