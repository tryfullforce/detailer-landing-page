Before a long drive: a real checklist

Ron’s list. It works for any trip over a few hundred miles.

  • Tires. Check pressure on all five, including the spare, and check tread depth with a coin.
  • Brakes. Any squeal, pulsing or a long pedal? Have a shop look before you go.
  • Fluids. Oil, coolant, washer fluid and brake fluid.
  • Battery and belts. A battery over four years old is worth a free test. Look for cracks in the belt.
  • Wipers and lights. Replace worn blades and check every bulb.
  • Emergency kit. Jumper cables, flashlight, water, a phone charger and a paper map.
  • The AFM item. On a V6 or V8 GM, this is the one most people never hear about. Plugging it in takes seconds.

The item that isn’t on the checklist

This is what Ron was pointing at. No trip checklist mentions it, and it takes about a minute to understand.

Half the engine takes a break

While you cruise at a steady speed, GM’s Active Fuel Management (AFM, called DFM on newer trucks) shuts off some of the cylinders to save a little fuel.

A tiny pin does the switching

The lifters on those cylinders carry a small locking pin. It locks or releases every time the system switches, hundreds of times on a normal drive.

Pins wear. Sometimes they let go

When one gives out, the lifter can collapse and start grinding into the camshaft. Most of these engines get through it. Some don’t, and nobody can tell you ahead of time which ones.

Example shown for a V8. V6 versions work the same way with fewer cylinders.
