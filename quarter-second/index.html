The pin that switches half your engine

Since 2007, GM has built Active Fuel Management into its V8 trucks and SUVs. AFM, or DFM on the newer ones.

When you’re cruising light, the computer shuts off half the cylinders to save a little fuel.

The lifters on those cylinders carry a small locking pin. Locked, the cylinder works. Released, the cylinder rests.

The one moment the pin is allowed to move

A cam lobe is shaped like an egg. One side rises and pushes the valve open. The round part is called the base circle.

That pin can only move safely when the lifter is sitting on the base circle, where nothing is pushing on it. Anywhere else, the lifter is under load, and the pin can bind.

So every switch is a tiny window. The whole change from eight cylinders to four takes less than a quarter of a second. GM says it’s about two turns of the crankshaft.

Think of changing your grip on a rope while someone’s pulling it. There’s one instant it goes slack. Miss that instant and you drop it.

Your truck does that hundreds of times on a normal drive. Then it does it again going back to eight.

What makes the pin late

GM wrote down what slows the unlock. I didn’t make this list up.

From GM's own service bulletin (PIP4568Q)

Tick noise and misfires on the AFM cylinders (1, 4, 6 and 7) "may be the result of internal locking pin damage, which may occur if the response time of an AFM lifter unlocking event is decreased due to low oil pressure, oil aeration, internal engine sludge," or wear in the lifter, the VLOM, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe.

Read the bulletin on NHTSA's site
What GM's bulletin says can make the pin late
  • Low oil pressure
  • Air in the oil
  • Internal engine sludge
  • Wear in the lifter, the oil manifold, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe

Clean oil gives that pin its best chance of being on time. It doesn’t take the job off the pin.

A pin that’s a little late, a few hundred times a drive, for years, has a lot of chances to be late once. When it is, the roller stops following the lobe the way it should. That’s the dip my thumbnail found.

That repair commonly runs $4,000 to $8,000.
