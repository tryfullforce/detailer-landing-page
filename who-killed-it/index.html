Didn’t matter.

What this part’s job actually is

Then I ask what this part’s job actually is.

Somebody always gets there. It’s a lifter, it sits between the cam and the valve.

But on these engines it’s got a second job.

Since 2007 GM has built Active Fuel Management into its V8s. AFM, or DFM on the newer ones.

When you’re cruising, the computer shuts off half the cylinders to save fuel.

To do that, the lifters on those cylinders have a small locking pin that engages and releases.

Then I make them count. One trip to the grocery store, how many times does it switch?

They guess ten, maybe twenty.

It’s hundreds. Every drive.

GM has put out its own service bulletins on lifter failures in these engines.

From GM’s own service bulletin (PIP4568Q)

Tick noise and misfires on the AFM cylinders (1, 4, 6 and 7) “may be the result of internal locking pin damage, which may occur if the response time of an AFM lifter unlocking event is decreased due to low oil pressure, oil aeration, internal engine sludge,” or wear in the lifter, the VLOM, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe.

Read the bulletin on NHTSA’s site

Clean oil gives that pin its best chance. It doesn’t change how often the part is asked to switch.
