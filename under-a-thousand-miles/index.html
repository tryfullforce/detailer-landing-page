Every review told you how a GM truck drives at 500 miles. None of them told you what can happen to the smoothest part of that drive by 80,000.

I wrote a lot of those reviews, and I’m the one who called it seamless.

Here’s how it works.

Review week

A manufacturer lends you a truck for a week. It’s brand new, spotless, and it goes back Monday.

You write about how it feels. You don’t write about year four.

A few years ago I reviewed a Silverado and said the cylinder deactivation was imperceptible.

I meant that as a compliment. And it was true. At 500 miles you can’t feel the engine going between eight cylinders and four.

I figured that was the whole story.

The email from Joyce

Then a reader named Joyce emailed me.

She was polite about it, which somehow made it worse.

“You said it was seamless. Mine came apart at 78,000 miles and the shop wants $6,100. Did anyone ever ask what happens later?”Joyce, a reader

I hadn’t. Nobody in my business does. We hand the keys back.

So I spent a month doing the part of the job I skipped.

I talked to techs. I read the service bulletins GM put out on lifter failures in these engines. I watched somebody cut open a collapsed lifter on a bench.

Here’s what I found out.

The seamless part is the part nobody watches

Since 2007 GM has built Active Fuel Management into its V8s. AFM, or DFM on the newer ones.

When you’re cruising, the computer shuts off half the cylinders to save fuel.

The reason you can’t feel it is that the lifters on those cylinders have a small locking pin that snaps in and out. Hundreds of times a drive.

That’s the exact thing I praised.

It’s also the part with the most work to do, and it doesn’t tell you when it starts to wear.

So the thing that makes it seamless is also the thing nobody’s watching. Here is how one of GM’s own bulletins describes what can go wrong.

From GM’s own service bulletin (PIP4568Q)

Tick noise and misfires on the AFM cylinders (1, 4, 6 and 7) “may be the result of internal locking pin damage, which may occur if the response time of an AFM lifter unlocking event is decreased due to low oil pressure, oil aeration, internal engine sludge,” or wear in the lifter, the VLOM, the plastic lifter guide, the lifter bore or the cam lobe.
