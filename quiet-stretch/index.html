Mine just turned 67,000 miles. It’s a 2017 Silverado 1500 with the 5.3, and I write the odometer in my surveyor’s field book every time I fill up. It’s a habit from the job: if you write it down, you can defend it.

My brother in law Denny bought the same truck the same week I did. Same year, same engine, same trim. Last spring he called me from the shoulder of Route 30. 71,000 miles.

Two weeks earlier he’d mentioned a little tick at idle. Everybody online said it was normal. The shop found a collapsed lifter and a camshaft lobe worn flat. $5,900.

Denny keeps his truck clean and has the oil change receipts in a shoebox. He said, “It ran perfect right up until it didn’t.”

But all three of them, and me, were somewhere between 55,000 and 85,000 miles. Broken in, well looked after, past the first big services. The quiet stretch. Nothing is wrong yet, which is exactly why nobody does anything.

Here’s the limit, and it matters. A module can’t repair a lifter that has already worn. If your truck is ticking, shuddering or losing power, get it looked at first. What it can do is stop the switching from here on, so every cycle after that is one the pin doesn’t have to make.

Why 67,000 miles looked fine

A truck can sound perfect at any mileage. Here’s the system behind the number, in plain words.

Half the engine takes a break

While you cruise at a steady speed, GM’s Active Fuel Management (AFM, called DFM on newer trucks) shuts off some of the cylinders to save a little fuel.

A tiny pin does the switching

The lifters on those cylinders carry a small locking pin. It locks or releases every time the system switches, hundreds of times on a normal drive.

Pins wear. Sometimes they let go

When one gives out, the lifter can collapse and start grinding into the camshaft. Most of these engines get through it. Some don’t, and nobody can tell you ahead of time which ones.

Example shown for a V8. V6 versions work the same way with fewer cylinders.

The pin counts cycles, not miles. Miles are just the easiest way to see how many you’ve stacked up.
